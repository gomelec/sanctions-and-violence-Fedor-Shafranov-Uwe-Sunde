## Headline findings

1. **The data allow a VAR in levels.** All series are stationary; persistence is well below 1 (03). All
   estimated VARs are stable (06).
2. **Violence → sanctions is robust.** Senders impose new sanctions after conflict. This holds in the Granger
   tests without controls (04), the lag grid, the response functions (07) and 11 of 12 IRF versions (08);
   also in the LP-DiD extension (+12.8 pp in the onset year, 12). It weakens with controls, mainly through GDP growth (a likely bad control, decision D8).
   **Not reproduced with GDELT** (11): insignificant in all OLS versions; but the GDELT incidence measure tracks
   UCDP conflict poorly, so this is not decisive.
3. **Sanctions → violence: a small, significant reduction in conflict two years later — conditional on
   controls and timing.** With controls, a new sanction lowers the conflict probability at t−2 by 3.5 pp
   (p < 0.01; mean 0.23); the Granger tests reject (incidence p = 0.026, levels p = 0.018), and the jackknife and
   GMM give the same negative sums (04, 10); so do logit (APE −0.046, p = 0.040) and PPML on winsorised deaths
   (−16%, p = 0.002) (09). With deaths in levels (capped at p99) the Granger test holds
   (p = 0.036); the cumulative IRF (−0.8 deaths per 100k over 10 years) has the same sign but is insignificant —
   about 15–25% noisier than the log version; year-by-year responses significant at h = 3–4 (09, fig17). The cumulative IRF without a same-year effect is −0.077
   [−0.154, −0.006] (08). Caveats: without controls the incidence sum is ≈ 0; if sanctions may affect violence
   within the year, the IRF turns positive (08); violence peaks in the sanction year (07; also LP-DiD, 12).
   GDELT (11): incidence gives almost the same estimate (−0.032) but insignificant (p = 0.15); media-reported
   violence in levels *rises* after sanctions (+0.027, p = 0.027).
4. **Sanctions coincide with escalations.** Violence is elevated before, and peaks in, the sanction year (07).
   Annual data cannot separate who moved first within the year.
5. **Overall:** senders react to violence (UCDP); sanctions are followed by a small decline in full-scale conflict
   two years later, but not by less violence overall, and possibly by more low-level violence (GDELT). Not a
   symmetric cycle of violence; the sanction effect depends on controls and on the within-year timing assumption.
